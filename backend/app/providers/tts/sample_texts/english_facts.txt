Deep beneath the ocean surface lies a hidden world where sunlight never reaches and strange creatures create their own glowing light. The deepest known place on Earth is the Mariana Trench, which plunges nearly eleven kilometres below the waves. If Mount Everest were placed inside it, the famous peak would still sit more than a mile underwater. The ocean holds so much dissolved salt that if all of it were spread across the land, it would form a thick layer covering every continent. Scientists say we have produced better maps of the surface of the Moon than of our own ocean floor. Every year researchers discover remarkable new species living under crushing pressure and freezing cold, proving again that life can survive in the harshest places imaginable on our planet today.
